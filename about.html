About

Hi, I'm Joshua.

Personal

I'm a final-year Electrical & Computer Engineering student at the University of Cape Town. Outside of lectures and lab work, I play guitar, run, and draw — mostly as a way to switch my brain out of "debug mode" for a while, though I'll admit the problem-solving habit follows me everywhere, including onto the page and the fretboard.

I chose ECE because it combines the problem-solving I love with something a lot of pure problem-solving doesn't give you: a physical result. I like that a circuit either works or it doesn't, that a waveform on an oscilloscope either matches the theory or tells you exactly where you went wrong. Electronics turns abstract reasoning into something you can hold, measure, and improve.

Career Goals

Where I'm headed

My goal is to become a microchip engineer — designing the processors and hardware accelerators that push computing past its current limits. It's an interest that's shaped most of my recent project work: I've designed a RISC-V processor core from the datapath up, and built a custom hardware accelerator (a Coordinate Rotation Unit) integrated into a RISC CPU, validating both against golden test vectors in MATLAB.

Longer term, I want to work at the intersection of computer architecture and silicon design — building the physical hardware that tomorrow's software runs on, whether that's in high-performance computing, edge inference, or next-generation processor design.
